# Software-Development-Lifecycle
CS-250

How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by first determining what the user is trying to accomplish and then breaking those needs into specific requirements that can be developed and tested. User stories are helpful because they describe a feature from the user's perspective and explain both what the user wants and why they need it. During the SNHU Travel project, feedback from users was made into user stories for features like destination recommendations and travel preferences. that made it easier to understand what needed to be developed and to create acceptance criteria that could be used to check whether the feature was completed successfully.

How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach developing programs by breaking a larger problem into smaller pieces and working through them slowly one at a time. I like the Agile approach because it allows development, testing, and feedback to happen throughout the project instead of waiting until the entire program is finished. In future development work, I would like to continue using Product Backlogs, user stories, Sprint Planning, and regular communication. I also think Sprint Reviews and Retrospectives are useful because they provide opportunities to evaluate both the product and the development process and make improvements as the project continues.

What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

To me, being a good team member in a software development team means communicating clearly, completing your responsibilities, and being willing to help the rest of the team reach the goal. Team members should be comfortable asking questions when requirements are unclear and giving feedback when they identify a problem. Working through the different Scrum roles during the Travel project showed me that developers, testers, Product Owners, and Scrum Masters depend on each other. A strong team member should be dependable while also remaining flexible when priorities or requirements change.
